AI Search Visibility: Ranked #5, Zero Clicks

6 September 2026 · 5 min read · Khatib Designs

AI search visibility is not the same thing as ranking. One of our own products, Spotly, sat at an average position of about 5 on roughly 8,000 searches in Google Search Console and earned zero clicks — a page that was seen by thousands of people on the first screen of results and chosen by none of them. That gap is the most useful number we have looked at all year, and it explains why a lot of GCC businesses feel invisible online despite a site that technically ranks.

Rank #5 on 8,000 searches, zero clicks

Here are the actual figures from Google Search Console: approximately 8,000 impressions over the reporting period, an average position of about 5, and zero clicks. Not a handful. Zero. The page was indexed, eligible, and served on the first screen of results thousands of times, and not one person clicked it.

Visible but unclicked is the single cleanest diagnostic in search, because it separates two problems that most teams blur together. Getting ranked is a crawling, content and authority problem. Getting clicked at a good rank is a wording and intent problem. If you have impressions, Google has already voted for you. The humans reading the results have not. Those need completely different fixes, and spending months building more content when your real problem is the second one is how budgets get burned.

Why a page ranks and still earns nothing

Five causes explain almost every zero-click page we have audited for clients in Kuwait and the wider Gulf:

  • The answer was already on the results page. A growing share of searches now end without a visit, because an answer box, a knowledge panel or an AI-generated summary satisfies the query in place. This is the well-documented zero-click search pattern, and short factual queries are the most exposed to it.
  • Average position 5 is an average. A page at 3 for a few rare queries and 8 for thousands of common ones reports as 5. The average flatters you; the distribution is what earns clicks.
  • The title answers a different question than the query. Our page was written in product language. The queries were written in problem language. People scan for their own words and skip anything that does not contain them.
  • Informational query, commercial page. Someone asking how something works will not click a pricing or signup page, no matter how high it sits.
  • Nobody recognises the brand. At position 5 you are competing against names the reader already trusts. An unknown name needs a sharper promise to win the click.

What we changed, and what it cost

The work was small and unglamorous. We rewrote titles and meta descriptions in the exact phrasing of the top queries in the Search Console report, not in our internal vocabulary. We moved a direct, complete answer into the first forty words of each page, so the reader who does click is rewarded immediately and does not bounce. We split one page that was chasing two different intents into two pages, each with one job. We added a short FAQ block answering the literal long-tail questions people were typing.

Total effort: under two days. No new backlinks, no new tooling, no ad spend. That is the point worth taking away — the highest-return SEO work available to most GCC businesses right now is not producing more, it is repairing pages that already rank. Before you commission anything, open Search Console, sort your pages by impressions, and look at every row where clicks are near zero. That list is your brief. We take the same approach to measuring automation work, which we wrote about in AI automation results: report the number that can embarrass you, not the one that flatters you.

Arabic queries decide visibility in the Gulf

A second finding from the same data: our Arabic impressions were a fraction of what the market actually searches. Most Gulf sites publish English first and treat Arabic as a translation chore, which produces pages that read like machine output and rank accordingly. Arabic search behaviour differs in phrasing, in dialect versus Modern Standard usage, and in how questions are formed. A translated page inherits English keyword choices and therefore competes for queries nobody types.

If a meaningful share of your customers are more comfortable in Arabic, the Arabic version of a page needs its own keyword research, its own title, and its own answer — the same discipline we apply when building bilingual interfaces, as in our DWA pharmacy delivery product, where the Arabic experience was designed rather than converted. The same rule governs conversational surfaces: an Arabic AI chatbot that was built in English and translated will misread the way people actually ask.

The monthly check that takes twenty minutes

Open Google Search Console, set the date range to the last 28 days, and enable impressions, clicks, click-through rate and average position. Then look at three things only: pages with high impressions and near-zero clicks, which need better titles; queries where you sit between positions 8 and 20, which are close enough that a stronger page reaches the first screen; and any query where the intent clearly does not match the page it is landing on, which needs a new page rather than an edit.

Do that once a month and you will make better decisions than most agencies will make for you. When you are ready to move from measuring to building — assistants, automation, and AI products that carry real business weight — that is the work we do at Khatib Designs AI.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my page rank on page one but get no clicks?

Usually one of three reasons: the search result page answered the query without a visit, your title uses different wording than the query people typed, or the intent behind the query does not match the page they are being shown. Check the queries column in Search Console before changing anything on the page itself.

Is a high average position in Search Console a good sign?

Only alongside click-through rate. Average position blends every query a page appears for, so a strong rank on a few rare terms can hide a weak rank on thousands of common ones. Impressions with zero clicks is the signal that matters.

Do we need separate Arabic pages or is translation enough?

Separate pages. Arabic queries are phrased differently, so a translated page carries English keyword choices and competes for terms nobody searches. Give the Arabic version its own keyword research, title and opening answer.
