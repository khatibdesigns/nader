Arabic App Design Sprint Kuwait: Price Bands

10 November 2026 · 4 min read · Khatib Designs

If you are pricing an Arabic app design sprint in Kuwait, the answer is three numbers, not one: a flagship sprint at KWD 3,000 to 12,000, a design retainer at KWD 750 to 1,800 a month, and a seven-day bilingual landing page at KWD 950. Here is exactly what each band includes, and what it does not.

What an Arabic app design sprint costs in Kuwait

The Arabic app design sprint price in Kuwait comes down to three bands, and the band you need depends on what you want to walk away holding: a full product design, a monthly design capacity, or one page that converts. A sprint is a time-boxed run at a product decision rather than an open-ended engagement - the format is well documented as a design sprint - and we price it the same way: fixed scope, fixed fee, written exclusions. Arabic-first means the right-to-left layout is the primary design and English is the mirror, not the reverse, because bidirectional text quietly breaks layouts that were drawn in English first. If the product also needs an AI layer, that work sits on our AI services page.

Band 1: flagship app design sprint, KWD 3,000 to 12,000

This is the band for a real product: a bilingual app you intend to ship and support. The range moves with screen count and flow complexity, not with how long we can stretch it. A simple booking or ordering app with 15 to 20 unique screens sits near the bottom; a multi-role product with dashboards, approvals and an operations back office sits near the top. You get a written scope with the screen list before you pay anything.

  • Included: a discovery workshop with your decision makers, Arabic-first information architecture with an English mirror, a full UI kit, a clickable prototype of the main flows, Arabic microcopy, developer handoff specs, and two rounds of revisions.
  • Excluded: writing the code, backend and hosting, App Store and Play Console accounts, payment gateway merchant setup, long-form content production, illustration or 3D, and anything after handoff. Build cost and running cost are separate conversations, and so is app maintenance.

Band 2: design retainer, KWD 750 to 1,800 a month

The retainer is for teams that already have a product and keep needing design: new screens, Arabic copy passes, campaign assets, design QA on what the developers built. You buy a fixed number of design days a month and work a queue, one active request at a time. It is deliberately boring and deliberately cancellable with 30 days notice.

  • Included: an agreed number of design days a month, one active request at a time with a stated turnaround, Arabic and English copy review on anything we design, and design QA against the live build.
  • Excluded: development hours, paid media management, same-day emergency turnaround, unused days rolling into the next month, and strategy work that belongs in a sprint. If you would rather not hold a monthly commitment at all, our fixed-terms approach is explained in what we charge.

Band 3: seven-day bilingual landing page, KWD 950

One page, Arabic-first, live in seven working days. This is the right band when you need to test demand before funding a product, or when a campaign is already booked and the page is the only missing piece. The timeline holds because the scope is small and fixed - the detail is in our seven-day landing page write-up.

  • Included: one page in Arabic and English, a written hero and offer section, one lead form or WhatsApp call to action, analytics and event tracking, mobile-first layout, and one revision round.
  • Excluded: a multi-page website, e-commerce or checkout, CMS migration, custom illustration, ad creative, and a second page at the same price.

Three results we can point at

  • Four screens instead of nine to place a pharmacy order in Kuwait, after the Arabic flow was redrawn as the primary path rather than a translated copy - see the DWA pharmacy delivery case study.
  • A live quote on a map instead of a phone call for a print marketplace, which moved quoting out of the sales inbox entirely.
  • Bookings without a call centre for an on-demand car wash, where routing and scheduling were designed together instead of bolted on after launch.

This versus hiring a Dubai studio

A Dubai studio can do good work, and sometimes it is the right call. The honest differences are these: day rates are usually higher, UAE VAT is added, and anything in person means travel on someone's budget. Dialect is the bigger issue - Arabic copy written for a UAE audience reads slightly foreign to Kuwaiti users, and that shows up in conversion before it shows up in feedback. Payments differ too: a Kuwait product needs KNET handled properly from the design stage, not an afterthought. And your contract sits under Kuwaiti law with a local entity on the other side, which matters on the day something goes wrong - we wrote up what that document should say in app development contract Kuwait. Cheaper is not the argument. Being in the same market, the same time zone and the same legal system is.

Frequently asked questions

What is the price of an Arabic app design sprint in Kuwait?

Three bands: a flagship design sprint at KWD 3,000 to 12,000 depending on screen count and flow complexity, a design retainer at KWD 750 to 1,800 a month, and a seven-day bilingual landing page at KWD 950. Each one has a written scope and written exclusions before you pay.

Does the sprint price include building the app?

No. The flagship band covers design, prototype and developer handoff. Code, backend, hosting, store accounts and payment gateway setup are quoted separately, and maintenance is a separate line again.

Why is Arabic-first different from translating an English app?

Right-to-left layout changes navigation, icon direction, form alignment and line length. If the English design is drawn first and mirrored, those decisions were already made for the wrong language. Arabic-first means the Arabic layout is the primary design and English is the mirror.

Can I start with the landing page and upgrade later?

Yes, and most clients should. The KWD 950 page tests whether demand exists, and what it teaches about the offer and the Arabic copy carries straight into a flagship sprint if the numbers justify one.
