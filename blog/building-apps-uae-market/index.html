Building Apps for the UAE Market: What's Different

1 September 2026 · 4 min read · Khatib Designs

An app that does well in London or San Francisco won't automatically win in Dubai. The UAE market has its own languages, payment habits, devices and expectations — and getting them right is the difference between an app that feels native and one that feels imported.

The Emirates is one of the most connected, smartphone-heavy markets in the world, with a young, diverse, demanding audience. That's a huge opportunity, but it also means users notice when an app wasn't built for them. Here's what actually changes when you design and build for the UAE.

Arabic — and real right-to-left design

The most important difference is language. A large share of the UAE audience prefers or expects Arabic, and Arabic isn't just English with different words — it flows right to left. That reverses your entire layout: navigation, icons, progress indicators, form fields and animations all need to mirror. Done properly, an Arabic app feels effortless to a native speaker. Done as a last-minute translation, it feels broken. We treat bilingual EN/AR as a design decision from day one, not a checkbox at the end — the same principle we cover in building bilingual Arabic & English apps.

Local payments and trust

Payment expectations differ. UAE users expect familiar local options and, in many categories, cash on delivery still matters alongside cards and digital wallets. Trust signals matter too: clear pricing, visible support, and a polished checkout all affect whether someone completes a purchase. An app that only offers a foreign payment flow will lose conversions it didn't need to.

Devices and performance

The UAE skews toward newer, premium devices — but "everyone has a fast phone" is a trap. Your users are on a mix of hardware and network conditions, and a heavy, sluggish app gets deleted quickly in a market with this much choice. Fast load times, smooth scrolling and a small footprint aren't luxuries here; they're table stakes.

Habits and expectations

  • High expectations for polish. UAE users are used to world-class apps. Rough edges stand out.
  • Multilingual, multicultural audience. Beyond Arabic and English, your users come from dozens of backgrounds — clarity and simple design travel further than clever copy.
  • Mobile-first everything. Many users will only ever meet your business through the app, so it has to carry the full experience.
  • Speed to support. Quick, responsive customer service — increasingly via chat and in-app — is part of the product.

Localisation beyond language

Real localisation goes further than translating the words. Dates, times, numbers and currency should follow local conventions, and prices should show in dirhams by default. Imagery and tone matter too — photography, icons and examples that feel right for the Gulf build trust in a way that generic stock content never does. Even small choices, like which day the week starts on in a calendar or how a name field is structured, signal to a user that the app was made with them in mind. None of this is expensive to get right when it's planned from the start; it only becomes costly when it's retrofitted after launch. Thoughtful localisation is often the difference between an app users tolerate and one they recommend.

Compliance and the practical details

Depending on your sector, there may be data and regulatory considerations to handle properly, from how you store user data to category-specific rules. These rarely block a good product, but they do need to be planned in rather than discovered late. A partner who knows the region will raise them early.

The bottom line

Building for the UAE isn't harder — it's specific. Get Arabic, payments, performance and polish right, and you have an app that feels like it belongs in your users' hands. Frequently asked questions

Does an app for the UAE need to be in Arabic?

For most consumer and many business apps, yes — a proper bilingual Arabic and English experience reaches far more of the market. Arabic should be a native right-to-left design, not a translation added at the end.

Which platform matters more in the UAE, iOS or Android?

The UAE has a high share of iOS users, especially in premium segments, but Android is significant too. The right choice depends on your audience and budget — many products launch on both, or start on one and add the other.
