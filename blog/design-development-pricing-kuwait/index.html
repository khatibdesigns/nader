Design and Development Pricing in Kuwait (2026)

17 October 2026 · 5 min read · Khatib Designs

Design and development pricing in Kuwait is usually a single number with no scope attached, which makes two quotes for the same app impossible to compare. This page publishes ours instead: three fixed tiers, three fixed durations, three fixed prices in Kuwaiti dinars, and the same payment terms for every client.

Why two Kuwait quotes for the same app differ by four times

Design and development pricing in Kuwait is rarely quoted the same way twice. One studio prices a two-week design sprint. Another prices a year of maintenance and a backend nobody asked for. Both send one number and call it a quote, so the comparison a business owner is trying to make cannot actually be made. Our answer is to publish the tiers rather than negotiate them, and to state what sits inside each one and what moves you to the next.

Fixed pricing only works when scope is genuinely bounded. The standard literature on software development effort estimation explains why honest estimates are ranges, not points. We absorb that range inside the tier instead of passing it to you as an open hourly bill. If the scope changes, you see a new tier, not a surprise invoice.

The three fixed tiers

Tier 1 — UI/UX: KWD 450, 2 weeks. Complete product design for one app or one web product: user flows, wireframes, a full high-fidelity interface in both English and Arabic with right-to-left handled properly rather than mirrored at the end, and a handoff file any competent developer can build from. You own the files. A fair number of clients stop here and build in-house. Book a scoping call or send the brief to nader@khatibdesigns.com.

Tier 2 — plus iOS and Android build: KWD 1,850, 6 weeks. Everything in Tier 1, plus a production build for both stores from a single codebase: authentication, your core booking or ordering or catalogue flow, push notifications, analytics, bilingual content, and submission to the App Store and Google Play under your own developer accounts. This is the tier most first launches actually need. Book a scoping call or write to nader@khatibdesigns.com.

Tier 3 — plus backend and API: KWD 3,200, 9 weeks. Everything in Tier 2, plus your own backend: database, REST API, admin dashboard, roles and permissions, payment gateway integration (KNET, Tap or MyFatoorah), transactional notifications, and the integration points an AI assistant or automation layer plugs into later. Choose this tier when operations staff have to run the product every day, as in our pharmacy delivery build for Dwa, where the dispatch side mattered as much as the app. Book a scoping call or send requirements to nader@khatibdesigns.com.

The KWD 600 per month retainer

After launch a product is either maintained or it quietly rots: an OS update breaks a screen, a payment SDK deprecates, a store policy changes, and six months later the app no longer installs. The retainer is KWD 600 per month and covers OS and SDK upgrades, store compliance, bug fixes, analytics review, and a fixed allocation of design and development time each month for small improvements. It is month-to-month, not a twelve-month lock-in, and it is optional at every tier.

Retainers are also how incremental work gets priced sanely. If you later want an Arabic assistant, a WhatsApp flow, or an automation layer on top of what we built, it starts as retainer work and only becomes a project when it outgrows the hours. For how that pricing behaves on the AI side specifically, see our breakdown of AI automation pricing in Kuwait and our AI consultancy pricing in Kuwait. Book a retainer call or email nader@khatibdesigns.com.

Payment terms and what actually moves the price

Terms are the same for every tier: 50% on signature, 50% on store submission. Nothing in between, no milestone arithmetic, no retention. For Tier 1 the second half falls due on design handoff, since there is no store involved. This is a straightforward fixed-price contract, which means we carry the estimation risk and you carry the scope discipline.

Four things genuinely move a price above these tiers, and we will say so before signature rather than after: integration with a legacy ERP or an insurer or bank system that has no documented API; hardware, in-store devices or printers; a second distinct product rather than a second screen; and regulatory review cycles you do not control. Everything else — more screens, more languages, more polish — belongs inside a tier or inside the retainer.

Which tier fits your situation

If you have a validated service and an internal team, take Tier 1 and build it yourself. If you need something in both stores this quarter, Tier 2 is the shortest honest path. If staff will operate the product daily and data has to live somewhere you control, start at Tier 3 — retrofitting a backend later costs more than including it now. Before you commit to any of them, read why AI and software projects fail, because the failure pattern is almost always scope and ownership rather than price, and check the realistic project timeline against your launch date.

If what you actually need is the AI layer — an Arabic assistant, a customer-service agent, an automation that removes manual work — that is scoped separately on our AI services page, and it sits best on top of a Tier 3 backend.

Frequently asked questions

Are these prices fixed or starting points?

They are fixed. KWD 450 for UI/UX in 2 weeks, KWD 1,850 for UI/UX plus an iOS and Android build in 6 weeks, and KWD 3,200 for those plus a backend and API in 9 weeks. The price only changes if the scope moves to a different tier, and we say so before signature.

What are the payment terms?

50% on signature and 50% on store submission, for every tier. On the design-only tier the second half falls due at design handoff, since no store submission is involved.

Is the KWD 600 per month retainer mandatory?

No. It is optional at every tier and runs month to month. It covers OS and SDK upgrades, store compliance, bug fixes, analytics review and a fixed allocation of design and development hours for small improvements.

Do I own the designs and the code?

Yes. Design files are handed over at Tier 1, and apps are submitted to the App Store and Google Play under your own developer accounts. At Tier 3 the backend and database are yours.
