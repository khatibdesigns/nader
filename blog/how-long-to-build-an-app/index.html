How long does it take to build a mobile app?

20 May 2026 · Updated 25 November 2026 · 7 min read · Nader Alkhatib

A mobile app takes 4 to 6 weeks to launch when it is built on a ready-made base, 6 to 8 weeks for a focused custom first app, 2 to 3 months once customers pay, book and log in, and 3 to 4 months for a multi-branch app with AI features and integrations. No real app goes from idea to both app stores in two weeks.

Those ranges are what we quote and deliver at Khatib Designs, where we have designed and shipped iPhone and Android apps since 2015, from Kuwaiti marketplaces such as iWASH, DWA and PrintIt to our own apps, Spotly and LUMA. The rest of this guide explains where the time goes, and what moves a launch date in the Gulf.

App development timeline by type of app

Type of appWhat it usually includesTypical time
Ready-made app under your brandBookings, ordering or loyalty on a proven base, with your logo, colours and content4 to 6 weeks
Focused custom first app (MVP)A custom design and up to about five core screens6 to 8 weeks
App your business runs onOnline payments, customer accounts, bookings or orders, an admin dashboard2 to 3 months
Multi-branch or integrated appEverything above, plus loyalty, AI features and links to your existing systems3 to 4 months

Every row covers iPhone and Android together, in Arabic and English. Building the same app twice, once per platform, is the slowest and most expensive way to do it.

The phases of a custom app build

  1. Discover (1 week). Goals, users, the features that matter on day one, and a written scope, price and launch date.
  2. Design (1 to 2 weeks). Every screen designed in Arabic and English. You click through it and sign off before any code is written, because changing a design costs hours and changing built code costs weeks.
  3. Build (4 to 8 weeks). The apps and the backend, tested on real iPhones and Android phones, with a working preview every week.
  4. Store review and launch (a few days to two weeks). Submission to the App Store and Google Play under your own accounts.

Testing is not a phase at the end. It runs through the build, which is why a weekly preview matters: problems surface while they are still cheap.

How long App Store and Google Play review takes

Apple reviews most submissions within a day or two, but a rejection sends you back to the queue, and the most common rejections are avoidable: missing account deletion, unclear in-app purchases, placeholder content or a privacy label that does not match the app.

Google Play is quick for established accounts. A new personal developer account, however, must run a closed test with at least 12 testers for 14 days before it can publish to production. Publishing under an organisation account avoids that wait, so set up your company account early. Apple's organisation enrolment needs a D-U-N-S number for your company, which can take from a few days to a couple of weeks to issue.

What adds time in Kuwait and the GCC

  • Payment gateways. KNET, Tap or MyFatoorah approval depends on your commercial registration, a corporate bank account and a compliance review of the finished app or site. Aggregators go live in days to a few weeks; a direct KNET merchant ID can take 4 to 8 weeks. Start this in week one. Our KNET integration guide has the full sequence.
  • Arabic done late. Treating Arabic as a translation step at the end means re-checking every screen for right-to-left layout, numerals and text length. Designing both languages from the start costs far less time. See what Arabic actually changes in an app.
  • Content that is not ready. Menus, product photos, prices and legal text are the most common reason a finished app waits to launch.
  • Several decision-makers. When feedback has to go through three people, each design round takes a week instead of a day.

What speeds an app project up

  • A tight first version: launch with the features customers use every week, then add the rest from real usage.
  • One person who can say yes, and who replies within a day.
  • Store accounts, the payment gateway application and your content started in the first week, alongside design.
  • A ready-made base for standard features such as bookings, ordering and loyalty, instead of building them from scratch.

Why an app cannot be built in two weeks

Design sign-off, a real build tested on devices and two store reviews do not fit in two weeks, even for a small app. Four weeks is the realistic minimum, which is why we quote 4 to 6. If someone promises a complete app in two weeks, ask what is being skipped: usually testing, Arabic, or the app being yours at all.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to build a mobile app?

A ready-made app under your brand takes 4 to 6 weeks. A focused custom first app takes 6 to 8 weeks, an app with payments, accounts and bookings takes 2 to 3 months, and a multi-branch app with AI features and integrations takes 3 to 4 months. Each covers iPhone and Android together.

Can an app be built in two weeks?

No. Design sign-off, building and testing on real devices, and two app store reviews take at least four weeks, which is why a realistic quote starts at 4 to 6 weeks.

How long does it take to build an MVP?

A focused custom MVP with up to about five core screens usually takes 6 to 8 weeks. On a ready-made base, a first version can be live in 4 to 6 weeks.

How long does App Store and Google Play review take?

Apple usually reviews an app within a day or two. Google Play is quick for established accounts, but a new personal developer account must run a 14-day closed test with at least 12 testers first, so publishing under an organisation account is faster.

What slows down an app project the most?

Scope that grows mid-build, slow feedback from several decision-makers, content that is not ready, and waiting on payment gateway approval such as KNET. Starting the gateway and store accounts in week one removes most of the delay.
