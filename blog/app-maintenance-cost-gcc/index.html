App Maintenance Cost in Kuwait & the GCC (2026)

28 October 2026 · 5 min read · Khatib Designs

Almost every quote you receive covers the build and goes quiet about year two. This is the app maintenance cost we actually quote in Kuwait and the GCC in 2026 — stated as monthly KWD bands, with the infrastructure bill underneath it and the factors that move it up or down.

What app maintenance actually pays for

Most owners budget the build and forget the twelve months after launch. An app is not a brochure site you can leave alone: Apple and Google ship a major OS release every autumn, your payment gateway rotates its SDK, a bank changes a field in the KNET response, and a dependency picks up a security advisory. Software maintenance is the formal name for that work — corrective, adaptive and perfective — and across the industry it absorbs the larger share of a product's lifetime cost. It is not a rounding error on top of the build.

In practice a maintenance month in Kuwait contains four things: keeping the app running (crash triage, store compliance, certificate and signing-key renewals), keeping it compatible (new iOS and Android releases, third-party SDK upgrades, Arabic and RTL layout regressions when you add a language or change a font), small changes the business asks for, and a human who answers when something breaks on a Thursday night. Quotes diverge mostly because a vendor has quietly dropped one or two of those four.

The app maintenance cost ladder in Kuwait and the GCC (2026)

These are the bands we quote, in Kuwaiti dinar, per month, for a single app with an existing codebase:

  • Keep-alive — KWD 150–300/month. Monitoring, crash triage, store compliance, certificate and key renewals, and one OS-compatibility pass a year. No new features. Correct for an internal tool or a stable, low-traffic app.
  • Standard maintenance — KWD 400–750/month. Everything above, plus 8–12 engineering hours a month for bug fixes and small changes, a named contact, and a one-business-day response target.
  • Active product — KWD 1,200–2,500/month. Two-week cycles against a real roadmap, analytics review, performance work, and continuous release. This is a part-time product team, not a safety net.
  • One-off compliance pass — KWD 600–1,500. For an app left untouched 12–24 months: dependency upgrades, store re-submission, and a written list of what is now unsupported.

The useful rule of thumb: budget 15–20% of the original build cost per year. A KWD 12,000 build should carry KWD 1,800–2,400 a year before you add any new features. Build bands are set out in app development cost in the GCC, and the full rate card sits in design and development pricing in Kuwait.

The running costs that sit underneath the ladder

Maintenance is labour. Infrastructure is a separate line and it is usually smaller than owners fear: hosting and a managed database run KWD 15–120 a month for most apps, push notifications are effectively free, transactional SMS and OTP cost roughly 10–18 fils per message, maps and geocoding run KWD 0–60, and store fees are USD 99 a year for Apple plus USD 25 once for Google. The number that actually hurts is an unbounded one — an unthrottled SMS flow or a map call on every scroll. Set a budget alert and a service-level objective before launch, not after; Google's reliability guidance is a sound free template for that. If the app includes AI features, the model bill is its own line with its own volatility — we break it down in AI running costs, and the delivery side of that work is on our AI services page.

What pushes your number up

  • Integrations. Each live third party — payments, ERP, courier, WhatsApp — is a thing that can change without asking you. Count them; they predict cost better than screen count.
  • Arabic and English together. Two content paths, two layout directions, and RTL regressions that only appear after a redesign. Real work, but cheap if it was built properly the first time.
  • Payments and anything regulated. More testing, more evidence, less freedom to defer an upgrade.
  • Abandoned code. No tests, no CI, unsupported SDK versions. This is the steepest multiplier we see, and it is why the first month on a rescued app is usually a fixed-price assessment rather than a retainer.

A concrete shape: DWA, the Kuwait pharmacy delivery app we built, carries almost none of its maintenance load in the UI. It sits in pharmacy stock integrations, courier routing and bilingual product content — the parts that change because the business changes, not because the code rotted.

And if you are a venue rather than an app owner, the honest answer is that the cheapest maintenance line is no app at all. Businesses taking bookings can list your venue on Spotly and let a platform absorb the OS upgrades, the store reviews and the payment SDK churn.

How to buy it without a retainer trap

Ask for five things in writing before you sign: the engineering hours carried each month and whether they roll over, the response target for a production outage, who holds the Apple and Google accounts and the signing keys (it should be you), what counts as a change versus a new feature, and an exit clause that hands back the repository and credentials within a fixed number of days. We price this the way we price everything else — fixed terms, no open-ended retainers — because a maintenance contract nobody can leave stops being maintenance quickly.

Frequently asked questions

How much does app maintenance cost per month in Kuwait?

Expect KWD 150–300 a month for keep-alive support, KWD 400–750 for standard maintenance including 8–12 engineering hours, and KWD 1,200–2,500 for an actively developed product. As an annual check, budget 15–20% of the original build cost.

Can I skip maintenance and only pay when something breaks?

You can, and for a stable internal tool it is sometimes rational. The cost is that an app untouched for 12–24 months needs a compliance pass of KWD 600–1,500 before it can be updated at all, and store removal for an unsupported SDK gives you no notice period.

Is hosting included in the maintenance fee?

Not in our pricing. Maintenance is labour; infrastructure is billed as it is used, typically KWD 15–120 a month for hosting and database plus per-message SMS and per-call map usage. Keeping them separate stops a vendor from marking up your cloud bill.
