What a Kuwait Kids Venue Gets From a Spotly Listing

20 October 2026 · 4 min read · Khatib Designs

A Spotly listing for a Kuwait kids venue costs nothing up front: you pay 12% of each confirmed booking on the host side, with a minimum of KWD 0.500 per booking, and your first three bookings are free. That is the entire commercial agreement — no listing fee, no monthly subscription, no setup invoice, no exclusivity. This page sets out what a venue actually gets for that 12%, what it has to supply in return, and how to tell inside one month whether the channel is earning its keep.

The terms, in one place

A Spotly listing runs on three numbers. 12% commission on the host side of every confirmed booking. A minimum of KWD 0.500 per booking, so very low-ticket bookings carry a floor instead of a percentage. And the first three bookings are free — zero commission — so your opening weekend on the platform costs nothing. Host side means the parent pays exactly the price you publish; the fee comes out of your share of the transaction rather than being added on top of theirs. That is deliberate: in a two-sided marketplace, the price a parent sees has to match the price on your own door, or the listing starts competing with your walk-ins instead of adding to them.

What the 12% buys

Three things, concretely:

  • A bilingual venue page. Arabic and English, written to be read on a phone by a parent deciding where to go in the next two hours — not a directory stub with your phone number on it.
  • Slot-based booking and payment. Your capacity per session, your cancellation window, payment collected at booking and paid out to you. A paid booking shows up far more often than a reserved one.
  • Discovery you are not paying for twice. Parents arrive through Spotly guide pages rather than through a paid ad, which is the only reason a 12% rate can work at all; an acquisition channel that costs you nothing until a booking completes is a different kind of expense from a monthly ad budget.

What it does not buy is traffic you already have. If a parent was always going to find you on Instagram and call you, do not route that booking through the platform — you would be paying 12% for a customer you owned. The listing is for the parents who do not know your venue exists yet.

What the venue has to supply

The listings that fail do so for operational reasons, not commercial ones. Before going live, you need: real capacity per slot (the number you can genuinely host, not the fire-code maximum); a cancellation window you will actually honour; your name, description and rules in both Arabic and English; all-in prices, including socks, entry for an accompanying adult, or anything else collected at the door; and a number somebody answers on Friday morning, which is when the bookings land. Closures and private hires have to be blocked out the day you know about them. A parent who arrives with a confirmed booking to a closed venue is a refund, a bad review, and roughly ten future bookings you never see.

Doing the arithmetic before you sign

Run your own numbers, because the KWD 0.500 floor changes the real rate at the bottom of the price range. On a KWD 6.000 session, 12% is KWD 0.720 and the percentage applies. On a KWD 2.000 entry, 12% is KWD 0.240, so the floor applies and you pay KWD 0.500 — an effective 25%. If most of your tickets sit under KWD 4.200, the floor is your real rate, and you should either price the Spotly slot as a bundle or accept the number knowingly. We would rather you see that before signing than discover it in month two.

Then judge the channel on incremental bookings only. Count bookings from parents who had never visited, multiply by your average spend per visit including food and extras, and compare that against total commission paid. Thirty days is enough to read the trend if you have blocked your slots honestly. Where the listing fits in your own operations

A booking channel exposes whatever is already weak behind it. Two patterns show up constantly in Kuwait family venues: slots sold without knowing which sessions actually fill, and staffing set by habit rather than by bookings on the calendar. Frequently asked questions

What does a Spotly listing cost a Kuwait kids venue?

There is no listing fee or subscription. You pay 12% commission on the host side of each confirmed booking, with a minimum of KWD 0.500 per booking, and the first three bookings carry no commission at all.

Why is there a KWD 0.500 minimum per booking?

Every booking carries a fixed cost in payment processing and support, which a percentage does not cover on very small tickets. In practice the floor becomes your effective rate on bookings under about KWD 4.200, so price low-ticket slots with that in mind.

Do I have to list all my capacity on Spotly?

No. Most venues start with a few sessions per weekend to test the channel. What matters is that the slots you do publish are real and honoured, including closures and private hires.

Can I keep my own bookings and walk-ins?

Yes. There is no exclusivity. Commission applies only to bookings that come through the platform, and you should keep sending customers who already know you through your own channels.
