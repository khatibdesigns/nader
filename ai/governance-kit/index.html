Your organisation already runs on AI. Who governs it?

Most organisations skipped the paperwork and went straight to the tools. That gap is now a risk you can name.

  • Your people are already using it

    Staff paste contracts, customer data and code into chat assistants every day, often on personal accounts. Without a policy, nobody knows what is allowed or who is accountable.

  • The law has started

    The EU AI Act's prohibitions and AI-literacy duty apply now, transparency rules since August 2026, and high-risk obligations from December 2027. ISO/IEC 42001 has made AI governance something auditors ask about.

  • Customers ask before they buy

    Enterprise buyers and procurement teams now send AI questionnaires. "We're working on it" loses deals. A documented programme wins them.

Which frameworks are the templates aligned with?

ISO/IEC 42001:2023, the NIST AI Risk Management Framework (with its Generative AI Profile) and the EU AI Act as amended in 2026, with GDPR touchpoints. Every template ends with a table mapping each section to the relevant clause, function or article. Aligned with does not mean certified: certification is an audit of how you run the system, not of your documents.

What format are the files?

Editable Microsoft Word documents (.docx) that also open in Google Docs, Pages and LibreOffice. The playbook is a PDF.

We are not in the EU. Is this still useful?

Yes. ISO/IEC 42001 and the NIST AI RMF are international and voluntary, and the templates are written for any organisation. The EU AI Act sections tell you when it applies to you, for example if you sell AI-enabled products or services into the EU.

Can I use the kit for my clients?

The licence covers internal use by the purchasing organisation. Consultants who want to adapt the kit for clients should get in touch for a practitioner licence.

Is this legal advice?

No. The templates are a practical starting point. Have qualified counsel review them against the laws that apply to you before adoption.
